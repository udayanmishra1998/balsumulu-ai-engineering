# Skill: Product Discovery

## Purpose
Guide the AI editor through structured product discovery — from raw idea intake to an approved product vision.

## When to Use
- A founder brings a new idea
- A project needs to pivot or re-examine its foundation
- Expanding into a new market or user segment

## Skill Steps

### Step 1: Idea Intake
**Goal:** Understand the raw idea completely.

Ask the founder these questions (adapt based on what they've already shared):

1. **The Problem:** What problem are you solving? Describe it as if explaining to someone who's never encountered it.
2. **The Evidence:** How do you know this problem exists? Personal experience? User conversations? Data?
3. **The Audience:** Who has this problem? Be specific — not "everyone" but a particular type of person or organization.
4. **The Impact:** What happens when this problem isn't solved? What's the cost (time, money, frustration)?
5. **The Alternatives:** What do people do today to solve this? Why isn't that good enough?
6. **The Vision:** If this product worked perfectly, what would change for your users?
7. **The Angle:** What's your unique advantage? Why are you the right person/team to build this?
8. **The Constraints:** Any hard constraints? (Timeline, budget, technology, regulations, geography)
9. **The Anti-Goals:** What are you explicitly NOT building?

**Output:** Document the raw idea as stated, noting what's clear vs. ambiguous.

### Step 2: Research Handoff
**Goal:** Define the research scope for the Research Analyst.

Based on the idea intake, formulate:
- The problem domain to research
- Specific competitors or alternatives to analyze
- Key questions the market research should answer
- Any specific data points the founder wants

**Action:** Pass these to the Research Analyst (or use the market-research skill directly).

### Step 3: Pain Point Mapping
**Goal:** Create a prioritized map of pain points.

After research is available:
1. List all pain points from founder input + research
2. Categorize by severity (critical / high / medium / low)
3. Categorize by frequency (daily / weekly / monthly / rare)
4. Map which existing solutions address each pain point (and how well)
5. Identify underserved pain points — this is where the opportunity lives

**Present to founder:** "Here are the pain points we've identified. Do these match your understanding? Anything missing?"

### Step 4: Target User Identification
**Goal:** Define who the product is for.

1. Identify distinct user segments from the pain point analysis
2. For each segment:
   - Who are they? (role, demographics, context)
   - Which pain points do they have?
   - How severe is the problem for them?
3. Recommend primary vs. secondary segments
4. Ask the founder: "Who should we build for FIRST?"

### Step 5: Value Proposition
**Goal:** Articulate why this product matters.

Using the validated pain points and target users, formulate:

**For** [target users]
**Who** [have this problem]
**Our product is a** [category]
**That** [key benefit]
**Unlike** [alternatives]
**Our product** [key differentiator]

**Present to founder:** Ask if this captures their vision. Iterate until it clicks.

### Step 6: Vision & Metrics
**Goal:** Define the product vision and success metrics.

1. Write a one-sentence vision statement
2. Define 3-5 success metrics (measurable, time-bound)
3. Document constraints and assumptions
4. List what the product is NOT

**Present to founder for final approval.** This is the Discovery gate — approval unlocks Requirements.

## Quality Checklist
Before declaring Discovery complete:
- [ ] Problem statement is crisp and specific
- [ ] Pain points are validated (not assumed)
- [ ] Target users are identified and prioritized
- [ ] Value proposition is clear and differentiated
- [ ] Vision statement exists
- [ ] Success metrics are defined
- [ ] Founder has approved everything

## Tips for the AI Editor
- Don't rush through questions — let the founder think and elaborate
- Summarize frequently — "So what I'm hearing is..." confirms alignment
- Challenge politely — "Have you considered..." surfaces blind spots
- Stay in problem space — resist jumping to solutions during Discovery
- If the founder is vague, ask for examples or analogies
- If the market research is concerning, present it honestly
