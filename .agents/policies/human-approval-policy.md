# Human Approval Policy

This policy defines when and how agents must seek approval from the founder before proceeding. The harness operates on a **gate-based approval model** — the founder must approve the output of each milestone before work moves to the next stage.

## Core Principle

**Nothing moves forward without founder clarity and approval.** Until the founder understands and approves the current stage's output, the next stage must not begin. This is non-negotiable.

## Milestone Gates

Each ADLC phase produces artifacts that require founder approval before the next phase begins. The milestones below are the default gates. At the start of each project, agents must **discuss these milestones with the founder** and adjust based on the specific project's needs.

### Phase: Discovery
| Milestone | Approval Required | What the Founder Reviews |
|---|---|---|
| Research findings complete | Yes | Market research, competitor analysis, pain points identified |
| Idea refinement complete | Yes | Refined problem statement, target users, value proposition |
| Product vision signed off | Yes | Vision statement, success metrics, constraints |

### Phase: Requirements
| Milestone | Approval Required | What the Founder Reviews |
|---|---|---|
| User personas defined | Yes | Who the users are, their pain points, their goals |
| Feature list drafted | Yes | Initial feature set with rationale |
| Requirements document complete | Yes | Full requirements with priorities and constraints |

### Phase: Specification
| Milestone | Approval Required | What the Founder Reviews |
|---|---|---|
| PRD first draft | Yes | Complete PRD ready for review |
| PRD finalized | Yes | Revised PRD with founder feedback incorporated |
| Acceptance criteria defined | Yes | Testable criteria for each feature/story |

### Phase: Planning
| Milestone | Approval Required | What the Founder Reviews |
|---|---|---|
| Product roadmap drafted | Yes | Phased roadmap with rationale |
| Epics defined and prioritized | Yes | Epic breakdown with priority order |
| User stories groomed | Yes | Stories with acceptance criteria, ready for architecture |
| Sprint/phase plan approved | Yes | What gets built first and why |

## How to Seek Approval

1. **Present the artifact** — Show the complete output, not a summary.
2. **Highlight decisions made** — Call out every decision the agent made and why.
3. **Surface open questions** — List anything unresolved or ambiguous.
4. **Recommend next steps** — What happens after approval, so the founder knows what they're unlocking.
5. **Wait for explicit approval** — "Looks good" or "Approved" or "Go ahead" from the founder. Silence is not approval.

## Blocking Approvals

The following always require approval regardless of milestone structure:
- Any change to the product scope or vision
- Any re-prioritization of the roadmap or backlog
- Finalizing any document that will be pushed to Confluence, Notion, or a project management tool
- Selecting or changing an integration or external tool
- Any action that is irreversible or affects external systems
- Deleting or archiving any artifact

## Partial Approvals

The founder may approve part of an artifact and request changes to the rest. In this case:
- Clearly mark what is approved vs. what needs revision
- Work on revisions and re-present only the changed parts with context
- Do not proceed to the next phase until the full artifact is approved

## Escalation

If an agent is blocked waiting for approval for more than one interaction cycle:
- Remind the founder what is pending
- Explain what is blocked downstream
- Offer to context-switch to other work that doesn't depend on the pending approval
